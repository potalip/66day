Phase 2 is over. You built the core craft of your track — and on the way you stopped being someone who "tries
coding" and became someone with a body of work. Today you look at it properly, then say it out loud.

## Today's ritual: the old-page test

Open your 66-day promise page from day 2 in the browser and press **Ctrl+U** (Cmd+Option+U on a Mac) to see its
source. Five weeks ago this was a wall of symbols. Now read it top to bottom and explain each block to yourself in a
sentence: "that's the heading, that's a CSS rule, that script does nothing useful." You can read it. Nobody taught
you HTML in a classroom — you absorbed it by shipping. Sit with that for a minute.

## 1. Portfolio seeds (20 min)

On day 57 you'll build your portfolio site. It starts today, with three choices. List your 3 best pieces so far
and, for each, one line on *why it's good* — not what it is.

- Apps examples: the day-27 main app ("three strangers used it and one came back"), the payments flow from day 32
  ("unhappy paths handled, I tested a declined card"), the day-31 automation that pings you on Telegram.
- Video examples: the day-27 explainer ("100% code, script to captions"), the day-33 one-command pipeline ("a topic
  in, an mp4 out in four minutes"), the day-34 auto-thumbnails.

Mark the one that's *almost* great. Phase 3 is where it gets real users or a real client.

## 2. "Five weeks ago I couldn't…" (15 min)

Write a short post with three honest "couldn't → can" lines, one thing that was harder than expected, and one
picture. Post it on your build log, X, Threads or LinkedIn.

```text
Five weeks ago I couldn't open a terminal without googling it.
Today I deployed an app with logins, a database and test-mode payments.
Five weeks ago I thought "API" was a kind of beer.
Hardest part: learning to say "no, smaller" to my own ideas.
```

> **Tip:** specific beats impressive. "I fixed a bug by reading the error first" lands better than "I'm now a
> full-stack developer".
