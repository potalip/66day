Yesterday you found ten real jobs. Today you knock on five doors. Most won't answer — that's the normal maths of
pitching, not a verdict on you. One reply from five is a good day for a beginner. The only way to get zero is to
send zero.

## What gets a proposal answered

Clients skim dozens of messages that start "Dear Sir, I am a passionate developer with strong skills". Yours
stands out by being about *them*:

1. **First line: their problem, in their words.** "You need customers to book and get reminded without you
   answering DMs at night."
2. **One proof, matched to their problem.** A link to the case study or demo from day 58 that's closest to what
   they asked. One link, not your whole site.
3. **A tiny plan.** Three steps showing you understood: "1) bot with your services and slots, 2) reminders the day
   before, 3) a weekly summary to you."
4. **One smart question.** "Do clients usually book one service or several at once?" It shows you think, and it
   invites a reply.
5. **Price and timeline**, if the posting allows — from your day-59 offer.

Keep it 80–150 words. Shorter than you think.

## Step 1 — Write and send five (45 min)

Pick five from your day-59 table. Mix them: for example two platform postings, two local businesses or cold emails,
one creator. Use the agent as an editor, not a ghostwriter:

```text
Goal: help me write a proposal for this job, 80–150 words.
Context: the posting: (paste). My best matching proof: (link to case study or demo). My offer and price: (paste).
Process: first ask me 2 questions about the job I should think through. Then draft it:
line 1 their problem in their words, then my one proof, a 3-step plan, one question, price and timeline.
Constraints: no clichés ("passionate", "I hope this finds you well", "I'm the perfect fit"), no bullet walls, sound like a person. Don't invent experience I don't have.
Done when: I can read it aloud in under 45 seconds and it couldn't be sent to any other client.
```

Then rewrite at least one sentence in your own words. Never send the same text twice — clients and platforms both
notice.

Two examples of strong opening lines:

```text
Apps: Saw that your clinic's booking form doesn't load on phones — I built a booking bot for a similar studio last month (40-second demo: link). Here's how I'd do yours in 5 days.
```

```text
Video: Your podcast has 80 episodes and no shorts yet. I cut one of them into a captioned 30-second clip to show you what I mean (link). I can do 5 per episode in 3 days.
```

Cold messages stay one-to-one: one business, one specific message, easy to ignore. Never bulk-send the same text to
a scraped list — that's spam, and in many countries it's also illegal.

Track every send in a small table: date, where, link, status, follow-up date. Follow up **once**, after 3–4 days,
with one friendly line.

> **If you're stuck:** if you've been staring at proposal #1 for 20 minutes, send it. The fifth will be twice as
> good as the first, and you only get to the fifth by sending the first.

## Step 2 — One platform profile (25 min)

Choose one: Upwork or Contra. The headline is your offer sentence from day 59. Add a real photo, 3 portfolio
pieces with the case study and demo, your fixed-price package, and one line on how you work ("I build with AI
coding agents, so I ship fast and you get the code"). Some platforms review new profiles; if yours is pending,
paste the profile text instead of the link.

## Step 3 — What scared you? (10 min)

Write a few honest lines: what did you fear before pressing *Send*? What actually happened? Any reply — even a
"no" — counts as data.
