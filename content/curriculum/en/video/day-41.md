Everything from the last two weeks — AI clips, code editing, the screenwriter, the brand kit, the pipeline, formats,
publishing, the app — was rehearsal for today. A real person with a real business needs videos, and you make them. Not
a perfect agency job: a clear brief, three solid videos on time, a clean delivery and an honest quote. That's how a
portfolio starts.

## Find the client (do this first, today)

Who to ask: a local business (café, gym, repair shop, tutor, yoga studio), a creator who needs shorts from their
long videos, a nonprofit, a friend's side project, a colleague's team at work. You want someone who will actually use
the videos.

The offer, in your own words: *"I'm learning to make videos with code and AI. I'd like to make you a pack of three — one
explainer and two shorts — for free (or a small fee) in exchange for honest feedback and permission to show it in my
portfolio."* Free pilots are fine. Unclear pilots are not: the brief fixes the scope.

> **Important:** if you can't land anyone by midday, pick a real business you know and make the pack as a spec piece:
> brief from their public site, then send it to them as a gift. Say it's unsolicited. The feedback task still needs a
> real reaction — no inventing quotes.

## Step by step

### 1. Discovery (20 minutes) and the brief

Do a 15–20-minute call or chat using your day-39 brief template. The questions that matter most: what should viewers
*do*, where will the videos live, what must be said, what's off-limits, what assets they have, by when. Then send the
filled brief back and ask "Is this right?" before producing anything. Paste the brief as proof (remove private details
like phone numbers).

### 2. Production (about 90 minutes)

A typical pack: **one 45–60-s explainer (16:9)** for the website or YouTube and **two 15–25-s shorts (9:16)** for
Reels/TikTok/Shorts. Run it through your tools:

```text
Goal: produce a 3-video client pack with my pipeline.
Context: client brief below; my make-video pipeline, brand kit, series template and formats flag (days 32–40). If my day-40 app works, submit the jobs through it instead of the command line.
Steps:
1. Create brand/client-<name>.json from the client's logo and colours (check contrast; use a licensed font).
2. Generate storyboards: one explainer (45-60 s, 16:9) and two shorts (15-25 s, 9:16) as a mini-series with the same hook structure. Use only facts and claims from the brief; mark anything else as [CHECK].
3. Export the three scripts as one readable script.md (scene, voiceover, on-screen text, visual) for client approval.
STOP after step 3 and wait for me.
4. After approval: render all three with captions, music at -14 LUFS, and 3 thumbnail options each.
5. Build the delivery folder: deliver/<client>/ with 01-explainer-16x9.mp4, 02-short-a-9x16.mp4, 03-short-b-9x16.mp4, matching .srt files, thumbs/, and README.txt (what's inside, specs, music and AI-footage licences, AI disclosure text).
Done when: deliver/<client>/ exists with all files and the README.
```

Send `script.md` to the client *before* you render. Ten minutes of their time on a script saves an hour of re-renders
later. Upload the folder to a shared drive (or an unlisted playlist) and paste the link.

> **If you're stuck:** if the client is slow to approve, render v1 anyway from your best guess, and clearly mark it
> "draft for feedback". If the client's logo is a blurry JPG, ask for the original or an SVG; meanwhile use their name
> in your display font — never upscale a logo into mush.

> **Safety:** never put the client's customers, staff or children on screen without their consent, never generate a
> fake person "testifying" for the business, and disclose AI footage in the README so the client can disclose it too.

### 3. Delivery and feedback

Delivery is part of the product. Send one message with the folder link, one line per video (where to use it), and
the question: *"What would you change, and what would you tell a friend about working with me?"* Do one revision round
if they ask. Paste their feedback **verbatim** — the good and the awkward — and one sentence about what you'd do
differently.

If they reply tomorrow, paste it tomorrow. A real quote beats a fast one.

### 4. Case study

Write a short public post (X, LinkedIn, Threads, your build log): the client's problem in one line, what you delivered
(3 videos, formats), how (your pipeline — one screenshot of the app or the terminal is gold), time spent, the client's
quote, and one thing you learned. Ask the client before you name them or show the videos. Paste the link.

```text
Draft a 150-word case study post from my notes below. Structure: problem → what I made → how (pipeline in one line)
→ time → client quote → one lesson. No hype words, no emojis, first person. Then give me 3 alternative first lines.
```
