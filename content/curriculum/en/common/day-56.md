Your capstone is out in the world: a public beta for apps people, a premiere for video people. It's collecting
clicks, views and opinions while you sleep. Today you don't fix anything. You listen, breathe, and say thank you.

## Today's ritual: read their words out loud

Collect every piece of feedback from the last few days into one file — `feedback.md` in your capstone repo works.
Tester messages, comments, the quote from yesterday's boss, the numbers (apps: sign-ups, how many reached the
"aha" moment; video: views, average view duration, where retention drops).

Now read the people part **out loud**. It feels silly for ten seconds. Then you notice things your eyes skipped:
the same word coming up twice ("confusing", "too long"), the compliment you dismissed, the question nobody should
have had to ask.

## 1. Working / not working (15 min)

Two columns, three lines each, no more:

- **Working** — e.g. "4 of 6 testers finished onboarding without help", or "people rewatch the 0:20 chart moment".
- **Not working** — e.g. "nobody finds the export button", or "45% gone by 0:08 — my hook is a slow logo".

Under it, one sentence: *the single change that would help most before launch.* That goes into days 61–64.

## The small surprise: one thank-you

Send one beta tester or viewer a personal note. Not a template — one specific line:

```text
Hey Sam — you were the one who said the export button hid behind the menu. I moved it today. Thank you for
actually using the thing and telling me.
```

People rarely hear that their feedback changed something. The ones who do often become your first real fans.

## 2. Beta progress post (10 min)

Share where you are: what's live, one real number, one thing you learned, what's next. Honesty works better than
hype — "3 of 10 people got stuck on step two, fixing it this week" earns more trust than "launching soon".

## You're done when…

- [ ] You pasted your working / not working lists and the one change that matters most.
- [ ] You pasted the link to your beta progress post.

Next four days: your portfolio and your first paid work — while the beta keeps running.
